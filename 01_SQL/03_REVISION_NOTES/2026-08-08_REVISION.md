## 🧠 STEP 1 — QUICK RECALL

### ❓ Recall Questions
1. In a question stating "Ignore employees aged below 25", what should your `WHERE` clause condition be?
2. In a question stating "Minimum experience is at least 2 years", what is the aggregate function and condition?
3. How do you sort output by `avg_salary` descending, and then by `dept` alphabetically ascending?

### 🗝️ Answers & Explanations
1. **Age Threshold (The Tricky Negative)**: "Ignore below 25" is a tricky way of saying "Keep everyone 25 and older!" $\rightarrow$ `WHERE age >= 25`.
2. **Min Experience**: "Minimum experience at least 2" means the lowest value in the bucket must be 2 or higher $\rightarrow$ `HAVING MIN(exp) >= 2`.
3. **Multi-Column Sort (The Tie-Breaker)**: Just like sorting a spreadsheet! Sort by money first, and if two people have the same money, use the department name as a tie-breaker $\rightarrow$ `ORDER BY avg_salary DESC, dept ASC;`

## 📖 STEP 2.5 — VOCABULARY & DEFINITIONS

- **Multi-condition**: Checking several different rules at the exact same time using `AND` or `OR`.
- **Descending (DESC)**: Sorting numbers from highest to lowest (e.g., $100 -> $1), or text from Z to A.
- **Ascending (ASC)**: Sorting numbers from lowest to highest, or text alphabetically from A to Z.
- **Threshold**: The exact boundary number in a rule (e.g., the threshold in `> 5000` is 5000).

## 💻 STEP 3 — SQL SYNTAX & CODE EXAMPLES

### 1. Mega Queries (Combining Everything)
```sql
-- Example A: The Ultimate Scenario 6 Boss Query
SELECT dept,
       COUNT(empid) AS employee_count,
       AVG(salary)  AS avg_salary,
       MAX(salary)  AS max_salary,
       MIN(exp)     AS min_experience
FROM emp
WHERE salary >= 45000                    -- Step 1 & 2: Get data and filter rows
GROUP BY dept                            -- Step 3: Sort into department buckets
HAVING COUNT(empid) BETWEEN 5 AND 10     -- Step 4: Check the buckets!
   AND AVG(salary) > 70000
   AND MAX(salary) > 120000
   AND MIN(exp) >= 2
ORDER BY avg_salary DESC, dept ASC;      -- Step 5 & 6: Label and Sort

-- Example B: Multi-Condition HAVING with Tie-Breaker ORDER BY (New!)
SELECT dept,
       COUNT(empid) AS emp_cnt,
       AVG(salary)  AS avg_sal
FROM emp
WHERE salary > 40000
GROUP BY dept
HAVING COUNT(empid) >= 6 
   AND AVG(salary) > 75000 
   AND MAX(exp) > 10
ORDER BY avg_sal DESC, dept ASC;
```

### 2. Tricky Logic Variations
```sql
-- Example A: Finding the lowest value to represent "Minimum Experience" (New!)
SELECT dept, MIN(exp) as Lowest_Exp
FROM emp
GROUP BY dept
HAVING MIN(exp) >= 3; -- If the lowest is 3, that means EVERYONE has at least 3!

-- Example B: Multi-column Tie-Breakers (New!)
SELECT name, city, salary
FROM emp
ORDER BY city ASC, salary DESC, name ASC;
-- Sorts cities A-Z. Inside that, highest salaries first. If two salaries are identical, sort names A-Z!
```

## ⚠️ STEP 5 — COMMON MISTAKES & DEBUGGING

### Mistake: Putting the Blender in the Bouncer's Hand
```sql
-- ❌ BUGGY QUERY: You can't use AVG() inside a WHERE clause!
SELECT dept, AVG(salary)
FROM emp
WHERE AVG(salary) > 70000
ORDER BY average salary DESC;
```
- **✔️ CORRECTION**: Math goes to the VIP Bouncer (`HAVING`), and names with spaces need brackets or no spaces!
```sql
SELECT dept, AVG(salary) AS avg_salary
FROM emp
GROUP BY dept
HAVING AVG(salary) > 70000
ORDER BY avg_salary DESC;
```
