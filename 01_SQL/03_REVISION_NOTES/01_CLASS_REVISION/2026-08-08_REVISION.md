## STEP 1 — QUICK RECALL

### Recall Questions
1. In a question stating "Ignore employees aged below 25", what should your `WHERE` clause condition be?
2. In a question stating "Minimum experience is at least 2 years", what is the aggregate function and condition?
3. How do you sort output by `avg_salary` descending, and then by `dept` alphabetically ascending?

### Answers & Explanations
1. **Age Threshold**: "Ignore below 25" means keep age 25 and above $\rightarrow$ `WHERE age >= 25`.
2. **Min Experience**: "Minimum experience at least 2" $\rightarrow$ `HAVING MIN(exp) >= 2`.
3. **Multi-Column Sort**: `ORDER BY avg_salary DESC, dept ASC;`

### Detailed Analysis of Today's 6 Scenarios

#### Scenario 1: Finance Bonus Eligibility
- **Row Filter**: `WHERE salary > 40000`
- **Group Filter**: `HAVING COUNT(empid) >= 6 AND AVG(salary) > 75000 AND MAX(exp) > 10`
- **Sort**: `ORDER BY avg_salary DESC`

#### Scenario 2: City Performance (Mistake Breakdown)
- **Common Mistake**: Writing `WHERE age < 25` instead of `WHERE age >= 25`, or forgetting `GROUP BY city`.

#### Scenario 6: Most Tricky Department Query
```sql
SELECT dept,
       COUNT(empid) AS employee_count,
       AVG(salary)  AS avg_salary,
       MAX(salary)  AS max_salary,
       MIN(exp)     AS min_experience
FROM emp
WHERE salary >= 45000
GROUP BY dept
HAVING COUNT(empid) BETWEEN 5 AND 10
   AND AVG(salary) > 70000
   AND MAX(salary) > 120000
   AND MIN(exp) >= 2
ORDER BY avg_salary DESC, dept ASC;
```

## STEP 3 — SQL SYNTAX & CODE EXAMPLES

```sql
-- Multi-Condition HAVING with Multi-Column ORDER BY
SELECT dept,
       COUNT(empid) AS emp_cnt,
       AVG(salary)  AS avg_sal
FROM emp
WHERE salary > 40000
GROUP BY dept
HAVING COUNT(empid) >= 6 
   AND AVG(salary) > 75000 
   AND MAX(exp) > 10
ORDER BY avg_sal DESC, dept ASC;
```

## STEP 5 — COMMON MISTAKES & DEBUGGING

### Buggy Query
```sql
-- Bug 1: Using WHERE for aggregate AVG(salary)
-- Bug 2: Invalid ORDER BY alias syntax 'ORDER BY average salary DESC'
SELECT dept, AVG(salary)
FROM emp
WHERE AVG(salary) > 70000
ORDER BY average salary DESC;
```
- **Correction**: Move `AVG(salary)` to `HAVING`, assign a clean alias in `SELECT`, and sort by that alias in `ORDER BY`.
```sql
SELECT dept, AVG(salary) AS avg_salary
FROM emp
GROUP BY dept
HAVING AVG(salary) > 70000
ORDER BY avg_salary DESC;
```
